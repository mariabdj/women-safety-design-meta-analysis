## 1. Introduction
1.1 Why women's perceived safety in public space matters (use of space, mobility, health, the right to the city) `[TO DRAFT]`
1.2 What design is thought to do: prospect–refuge, natural surveillance / "eyes on the street", CPTED, broken windows / incivilities, lighting and reassurance `[TO DRAFT]`
1.3 What existing syntheses show and what they leave open `[TO DRAFT]` (sources: `overlap_search/OVERLAP_REPORT.md`)
- Knabe et al. (2026, preliminary): physical environment interventions reduce fear of crime overall; gender tested only at study level (non-significant).
- Te Braak & van Tienoven (2025): narrative review of women's perceived safety, excludes women–men comparisons.
- Lorenc et al. (2013), Fotios et al. (2015), Dubey et al. (2025): narrative, no pooling.
1.4 The gap: no pooled, within-study comparison of women's and men's responses to the same design features `[TO DRAFT]`
1.5 Aims, research questions and hypotheses `[D1]` `[D2]`
- RQ1: For each design feature, what is the average effect on women's perceived safety?
- RQ2: Is the effect of each feature larger for women than for men in the same studies?
- RQ3: Is the women–men gap in perceived safety smaller in better-designed scenes?

## 2. Methods
Reporting: PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-S; protocol structure follows PRISMA-P and NIRO-SR (Topor et al., 2023). Checklist mapping in Appendix A.

|---|---|---|

2.2 Information sources and search strategy `[TO DRAFT]`: databases, Boolean blocks (from the scoping search, version 3, validated on 5 benchmark reviews), grey literature, citation chasing, Plan C sources (guide Step 2b); full strings in Appendix B; search dates.
2.3 Study selection `[TO DRAFT]`: deduplication; ASReview with stopping rule `[D8]`; full-text screening with fixed exclusion reasons; solo-author safeguards (independent AI second screener on 100%, author re-screening of 20% after ≥ 1 week, Cohen's κ for both, author decides all disagreements).
2.4 Data extraction `[TO DRAFT]`: frozen extraction sheet (variables in Appendix C); 100% independent double extraction; SD/SE rule; figure digitising with WebPlotDigitizer; author contact procedure and deadlines.
2.5 Plan C: evidence beyond journal articles `[TO DRAFT]`: Tier 1 (open raw data, theses, proceedings, figures, author replies) and Tier 2 (official surveys, context only); five recognition rules including the sanity check.
2.6 Risk of bias `[TO DRAFT]`: adapted JBI quasi-experimental checklist, six items, overall-rating rule, use in sensitivity analysis.
2.7 Effect sizes `[TO DRAFT]`: Hedges' g with exact correction; SMD (between), SMCR (within, r imputed 0.5; sensitivity 0.3/0.7); OR→d; t→d; sign convention g > 0 = safer. Independent re-computation check.
2.8 Synthesis `[TO DRAFT]`: per feature, k ≥ 3 studies; three-level random-effects model (REML, t-tests, CR2 robust SE); gender moderator (RQ2); design-quality × gender (RQ3, k ≥ 10); prediction intervals; SWiM for k < 3.
2.9 Sampling plan / sensitivity analysis `[D2]` `[TO DRAFT]`: smallest effect size of interest; expected k; minimal detectable effect.
2.10 Heterogeneity, publication bias, sensitivity analyses `[TO DRAFT]`
2.11 Outcome-neutral checks (criterion 1E) `[TO DRAFT]`: e.g. the women > men baseline fear difference must replicate in included studies; search benchmark recall; effect-size re-computation agreement.
